Hi! I'm Amen from Ethiopia, a fourth-year Computer Science student at KAIST with a minor in Brain and Cognitive Sciences, graduating in February 2027.

I like building and studying systems that help people use information their senses or context can't reach, with a focus on accessibility and on how older adults and visually impaired users interact with AI. My interests span human-computer interaction, assistive technology and computational neuroscience, and I have done research in KAIST's Interactive Computing Lab, Make Lab and Brain Repair Laboratory, and in the Wormery Lab at the University of Leeds.

Research

Wizard-of-Oz mobile automation agent for older adults

Jul. 2025 – Current
Interactive Computing Lab (Prof. Uichin Lee), KAIST

Built the Android app system for a Wizard-of-Oz mobile automation agent used in the paper's user study with older adults, with an in-app floating chat overlay and a FastAPI backend with a wizard-side console.

  • Quantitatively analyzed smartphone interaction logs (delegation versus intervention, interaction rate, active interaction span).
  • Co-authored an IMWUT paper Speak, Watch, Verify: A Mental Model of Older Adults' First-Use Interactions with Mobile Automation Agents; under major revision, currently working on the resubmission.
  • Prototyped a RAG-based Q&A assistant for older adults on YouTube as a preliminary study that informed the Wizard-of-Oz study.

Spatial audio navigation under situational blindness

Jan. 2026 – Current
Make Lab (Prof. Andrea Bianchi), Industrial Design, KAIST

Building a Unity-based virtual reality system for spatial audio navigation in situations where vision is unavailable.

  • Designing VR study scenarios (path to exit, obstacle search) to test how far audio alone can support navigation, to inform navigation support for visually impaired people.
  • Previously built a node-based image generation interface around recursive pairwise synthesis.

Accessibility redesign of a mental-health app

Dec. 2024 – Feb. 2025
Batoners (CEO Lee Ji-su)

Redesigned an existing mental-health app for blind and deaf users, with haptic feedback, text-to-speech output, and visual aids.

  • Wrote accessibility documentation for the development team.

Neural circuit recovery after injury

Jul. 2026 – Aug. 2026
Wormery Lab (Prof. Netta Cohen), University of Leeds

Studied whether a neural circuit that loses part of its structure to injury can recover its timing using only the connections it still has, motivated by recovery after spinal cord injury.

  • Worked in a dynamical-systems framework, characterising limit cycles, phase-response curves and phase locking in pulse-coupled FitzHugh–Nagumo oscillators.
  • Found that, within the model, surviving connections alone restored the injured unit's firing rhythm, and discussed the model's limits for recovery after spinal cord injury.

Cellular profiling for Parkinson's drug discovery

Mar. 2024 – Dec. 2024
Brain Repair & Organoid Intel Lab (Prof. Minee Choi), KAIST

Conducted research on induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) and their applications in personalized drug discovery.

  • Studied the reprogramming process of somatic cells into iPSCs using Yamanaka factors and analyzed methods for differentiating iPSCs into cortical neurons.
